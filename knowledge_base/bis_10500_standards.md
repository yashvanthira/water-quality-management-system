# Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS 10500:2012) Drinking Water Specification

## 1. Acceptable and Permissible Limits

The Bureau of Indian Standards defines mandatory limits for potable water supplied to citizens:

| Parameter | Acceptable Limit (Desirable) | Permissible Limit in Absence of Alternate Source | Health Impact of Exceedance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **pH Value** | 6.5 to 8.5 | No relaxation | Below 6.5: Corrosive to plumbing, metallic taste; Above 8.5: Encrustation, eye irritation. |
| **Dissolved Solids (TDS)** | 500 mg/L | 2000 mg/L | High TDS impairs palatability, damages kidney function, gastrointestinal irritation. |
| **Nitrate (as NO3)** | 45 mg/L (approx. 10 mg/L as Nitrate-N) | No relaxation | Methemoglobinemia ("Blue Baby Syndrome") in infants, potential carcinogenic risk with long-term exposure. |
| **Total Coliform** | Must not be detectable in any 100 ml sample (0 MPN/100ml) | Must not be detectable | Indicator of fecal pathogen contamination (cholera, salmonella, rotavirus). |
| **E. coli or Fecal Coliform** | 0 MPN/100ml | 0 MPN/100ml | Acute gastrointestinal illness, diarrhea, dysentery, dehydration. |
| **Electrical Conductivity** | ~750 µmho/cm | ~2000 µmho/cm | High conductivity reflects high dissolved ionic content and mineral salts. |
| **Turbidity** | 1 NTU | 5 NTU | High turbidity shields pathogens from chemical disinfection. |

## 2. Public Health Rule: Raw Water vs Drinking Water
- Raw river water or canal water is NEVER compliant with BIS 10500 without multi-barrier treatment.
- Class A raw water still requires disinfection (chlorination or UV treatment) before it can meet the 0 Coliform requirement of BIS 10500.
- Class C raw water requires full conventional municipal treatment (coagulation, flocculation, rapid sand filtration, chlorination) before tap distribution.
